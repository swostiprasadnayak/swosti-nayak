## SECTION 1: IDENTIFYING YOUR PRIMARY USER SEGMENT

You asked: "Who should we target?" Here's my recommendation process:

### Why You Need a Primary User
**Problem**: "Anyone" means infinite research scope. You'll never finish.

**Solution**: Pick ONE primary segment with 3 characteristics:
1. **High research urgency** (their pain is acute, not chronic)
2. **Accessible to observe** (you can spend time with them in their context)
3. **Defensible design rationale** (clear why their needs matter)

### Evaluating Your Candidate Segments

| Segment | Research Urgency | Observability | Design Defensibility | Recommendation |
|---------|------------------|----------------|----------------------|-----------------|
| **Health sector workers** (doctors, nurses, therapists) | HIGH - extreme stress, life/death decisions, emotional toll | MEDIUM - hospitals have privacy restrictions, shift work varies | HIGH - wellbeing directly impacts care quality | ✅ STRONG CANDIDATE |

| **Travelers/explorers** | MEDIUM - jet lag, disorientation | LOW - temporary states, hard to track | MEDIUM - compelling but niche | Secondary |

### My Recommendation: PRIMARY USER SEGMENT

**Go with CONSULTANTS (high-travel, multi-context professionals)**

#### Why?
1. **Research urgency**: They experience ALL your design challenges simultaneously:
   - Context switching (home → airport → client site → hotel → airplane → back home)
   - Stress cycles (pitch day → client meeting → travel fatigue → recovery)
   - Sensory disorientation (jet lag, unfamiliar spaces, constant environmental shifts)
   - Environmental optimization need (need to perform in unfamiliar rooms, hotels, client offices)

2. **Observability**: 
   - You can track their multi-context journey (not stuck in one space)
   - High travel means staying in different environments (direct sensory research)
   - Stress is acute and visible (easier to observe and measure)

3. **Defensibility**:
   - Underserved market (no existing smart ambient systems for travelers)
   - High-value segment (willing to pay for solutions)
   - Learnings generalize to other high-stress professionals
   - Unique olfactory opportunity (hotel room scents, travel fatigue, contextual scent memory)

#### Secondary User Segments

### PHASE 1: LANDSCAPE RESEARCH (Weeks 1-2)

#### A. LITERATURE REVIEW

**Domain 1: Cognitive Science & Performance**
- Research question: "How does environment affect cognitive performance?"
- Read: Circadian rhythm research, environmental psychology, attention restoration theory
- Key papers to find:
  - "Attention Restoration Theory" (Kaplan & Kaplan)
  - Circadian lighting research (Harvard Medical School)
  - Cognitive load & working memory (Sweller)
- Output: One-page summary on how lighting, sound, temperature, scent affect focus, memory, stress recovery
- Time: 3-4 hours

**Domain 2: Olfactory Science**
- Research question: "How does smell affect mood, memory, and performance?"
- Read: Olfactory neuroscience, aromatherapy research, scent perception psychology
- Key topics:
  - Olfactory bulb → limbic system connection (why smell is emotional)
  - Habituation & sensory adaptation (scents fade if constant)
  - Cross-modal sensory integration (how smell affects perception of color, sound)
  - Scent diffusion technology & challenges
- Output: Technical constraints document (what's hard about olfactory delivery)
- Time: 4-5 hours

**Domain 3: Biometric Mood Detection**
- Research question: "How reliably can wearables detect mood/stress?"
- Read: HRV (heart rate variability) research, stress detection algorithms, ML accuracy studies
- Key findings:
  - HRV accuracy: ~70-80% (not perfect)
  - Skin conductance: ~75% (affected by physical exertion, temperature)
  - Combining multiple signals: ~85% (still not reliable)
  - False positives create distrust (design implication)
- Output: Accuracy constraints document with design implications
- Time: 3-4 hours

**Domain 4: Privacy & Biometric Ethics**
- Research question: "What are user concerns with biometric tracking?"
- Read: Privacy regulations (GDPR, local laws), ethical guidelines for biometric data
- Key topics:
  - Data ownership & control
  - Local vs. cloud processing trade-offs
  - Consent & transparency standards
  - Emergency scenarios (when should system override privacy?)
- Output: Privacy requirements document
- Time: 2-3 hours

**Domain 5: Multisensory Experience Design**
- Research question: "How do multiple senses work together in immersive experiences?"
- Read: Aura research (you have this), multisensory therapy, VR/MR design principles
- Key findings:
  - Sensory hierarchy (visual 40%, aural 30%, tactile 20%, olfactory 5%, etc.)
  - Latency sensitivity (when do delays cause motion sickness?)
  - Cross-modal dominance (which sense takes priority in conflicts?)
  - Sensory adaptation (when do people stop noticing changes?)
- Output: Multisensory design principles document
- Time: 3-4 hours

**Total Phase 1 Time: 15-20 hours (1 week part-time)**

### PHASE 3: CONSTRAINT RESEARCH (Weeks 8-10)

#### A. OLFACTORY DEEP-DIVE

**Research Question**: "What are the technical, perceptual, and safety constraints of olfactory delivery?"

**Activities**:

1. **Hardware Vendor Interviews** (2-3 hours)
   - [ ] Contact: Osmall, Scentronomy, other scent delivery companies
   - [ ] Ask:
     - What's your latency (seconds between trigger and scent detected)?
     - How long until smell adapts/fades?
     - Maintenance burden (refilling, cleaning)?
     - Cost per unit? Scaling costs?
     - What about scent mixing? Lingering?
     - Allergy concerns? Safety data?
   - Output: Technical feasibility assessment

2. **Scent Perception Research** (4-5 hours reading)
   - [ ] Olfactory adaptation: How fast does scent become "invisible"?
     - Typical timeline: 15-20 minutes (olfactory receptor fatigue)
     - Implication: Can't use constant scent, need periodic intensity changes
   - [ ] Cross-modal effects: Does scent affect perception of other senses?
     - Yes: Scent can enhance/distract from visual/aural focus
     - Implication: Timing of scent release matters (before/after visual change)
   - [ ] Scent + memory: How does smell trigger emotional/memory associations?
     - Olfactory bulb connects directly to limbic system (emotional center)
     - Implication: Scent is most emotionally powerful sensory modality
   - [ ] Negative effects: Nausea, headaches, allergic responses
     - Implication: Scent must be optional, easily disableable

3. **Scent Selection Research** (3-4 hours)
   - [ ] Which scents support which cognitive states?
     - Focus: Rosemary, peppermint, lemon (alertness)
     - Calm: Lavender, chamomile, sandalwood (relaxation)
     - Energy: Citrus, cinnamon, ginger (invigoration)
     - Source: Scientific literature on aromatherapy + sensory psychology
   - [ ] Which scents are travel-friendly (hotel-appropriate)?
     - Avoid: Strong, divisive scents (people have strong preferences)
     - Use: Subtle, universal scents (light florals, citrus)
     - Implication: Scent palette should be refined, not heavy

4. **User Testing: Scent Sensitivity** (2-3 hours)
   - [ ] Test with 3-4 of your ethnographic participants
   - [ ] Present different scents in sequence, measure:
     - Emotional response ("Does this feel calming?" 1-10)
     - Appropriateness ("Would you want this in your hotel room?")
     - Adaptation speed ("When did you stop noticing it?")
   - [ ] Test different intensities (subtle → strong)
   - [ ] Document preferences (individual variation is high)

**Output**: Olfactory constraints document
- What's technically possible
- Perception/safety limitations
- Scent palette recommendations (5-7 core scents)
- Integration strategy (when/how to use scent without overload)

#### B. BIOMETRIC ACCURACY TESTING

**Research Question**: "How accurately can we detect mood/stress from wearables?"

**Activities**:

1. **Data Analysis from Ethnographic Phase**
   - [ ] Correlate biometric markers (HRV, HR, skin conductance) with:
     - Self-reported mood
     - Observed stress levels (video/notes)
     - Environmental changes
   - [ ] Quantify accuracy:
     - Can we detect "high stress" with >80% accuracy?
     - Can we detect "relaxed" with >80% accuracy?
     - What about nuances ("slightly anxious" vs. "moderately anxious")?

2. **False Positive Analysis**
   - [ ] When did system detect stress but user wasn't stressed?
     - Example: HRV low due to exercise (not emotional stress)
     - Example: Excitement at good news (elevated HR/HRV, not stress)
   - [ ] Document these cases: "Here's a false positive scenario"
   - [ ] Design implication: "System must ask confirmation"

3. **Literature Review: ML Accuracy**
   - [ ] Current state-of-art: ~85% accuracy with multi-signal fusion
   - [ ] Real-world challenges:
     - Individual variation (baseline HRV differs per person)
     - Context variation (same HRV can mean stress OR excitement)
     - Medication/health effects (coffee, illness, hormonal cycles affect HRV)
   - [ ] Implication: Design for ~80% accuracy (20% will be wrong)

**Output**: Biometric accuracy assessment
- What our system can reliably detect (with confidence threshold)
- What it struggles with (false positives, false negatives)
- Design requirement: "System must be transparent about confidence level"

#### C. SENSORY SYNC LATENCY MAPPING

**Research Question**: "What latency thresholds cause motion sickness vs. pleasant surprise?"

**Activities**:

1. **Latency Baseline Research**
   - [ ] Literature: When do humans perceive asynchrony?
     - Visual-aural sync: Noticeable lag >50ms (film industry standard: 80ms max)
     - Visual-thermal: Thermal changes slow (1-2°C per minute baseline)
     - Visual-olfactory: Smell registers in 5-10 seconds
   - [ ] Implication: Different modalities have different latency tolerances

2. **Test Multisensory Transitions**
   - [ ] With your ethnographic participants, test transitions:
     - Scenario: Change from "Work Focus" → "Stress Recovery"
     - Measure participant comfort with different timing patterns:
       - Pattern A: All simultaneous (visual + aural + thermal + scent at once)
       - Pattern B: Staggered (visual first, aural 5s later, thermal 10s later, scent 15s later)
       - Pattern C: Gradual (all smooth transitions over 30 seconds)
     - Ask: "Which felt most natural? Least jarring?"
   - [ ] Document: Preferred transition style

3. **Motion Sickness Testing**
   - [ ] Research: What causes motion sickness in multisensory environments?
     - Vestibular conflict (inner ear conflict with visual motion)
     - Sensory incongruence (senses saying different things)
   - [ ] For Surrounding: Unlikely to cause motion sickness (not moving body)
   - [ ] But: Sensory incongruence (cold air + warm light) might cause discomfort

**Output**: Sensory sync specifications
- Recommended latency budgets per modality combination
- Transition timing preferences (how fast is "natural"?)
- Motion sickness risk mitigation strategies

#### D. PRIVACY CONCERN INTERVIEWS

**Research Question**: "What specific privacy concerns exist with biometric tracking?"

**Activities**:

1. **Interview Questions** (30 min per person, with 8-10 participants)
   - [ ] "What data are you most concerned about being tracked?"
   - [ ] "Would you trust biometric data stays on-device? Why/why not?"
   - [ ] "If system makes wrong mood inference, what should happen?"
   - [ ] "Who should have access to your data? (you, device, therapist, insurance?)"
   - [ ] "What would make you trust an ambient system with your health data?"
   - [ ] "Would you prefer manual control even if it reduces system effectiveness?"

2. **Specific Scenarios**
   - [ ] "System detects you're anxious before client meeting. It forces deep breathing + calming environment. Okay with that?"
   - [ ] "System data could help identify depression patterns. Worth sharing with doctor? Therapist?"
   - [ ] "System learns your preferences over time. Who owns that learning?"

3. **Document**:
   - [ ] Privacy concerns by segment (consultants vs. others)
   - [ ] Trust-building requirements
   - [ ] Transparency needs
   - [ ] Control preferences (auto vs. manual)

**Output**: Privacy requirements document
- Must-haves: Local processing, user control, audit logs, kill switch
- Nice-to-haves: Consent granularity, data deletion, sharing options
- Design patterns: Transparency interface requirements

### PHASE 5: PROTOTYPING & VALIDATION (Weeks 13-15)

#### A. PRESET SCENE DESIGN

Based on your consultant research, design 5-6 core "scenes":

**Scene 1: "Arrival Calm" (Entering new hotel/client space)**
- Visual: Warm amber lighting (3000K), 60% brightness (not clinical)
- Aural: Subtle ambient music or nature sounds (45dB - quiet background)
- Thermal: 22°C (neutral comfort)
- Scent: Subtle vanilla or cedarwood (welcoming, not overpowering)
- Duration: First 30 minutes of session
- Trigger: User arrives at new location (manual trigger or AI-detected arrival)

**Scene 2: "Deep Focus" (Preparing for important presentation/meeting)**
- Visual: Cool blue-white lighting (4500K), 75% brightness
- Aural: White noise or binaural beats (40Hz for focus), 50dB
- Thermal: 19-20°C (cool for alertness)
- Scent: Rosemary or peppermint (subtle, for focus)
- Duration: 1-2 hours before meeting
- Trigger: Manual selection or calendar event detected

**Scene 3: "Stress Recovery" (After high-stakes meeting)**
- Visual: Warm, dimmed lighting (2700K), 30% brightness
- Aural: Nature sounds or instrumental (gentle), 40dB
- Thermal: 22-23°C (warmer for comfort)
- Scent: Lavender (calming)
- Duration: 30-45 minutes (typical recovery window)
- Trigger: Post-meeting mood check ("How stressed are you?" 1-10)

**Scene 4: "Sleep Prep" (Evening, for hotel rest)**
- Visual: Red-shifted light (below 1000K, candlelight-like), 5% brightness
- Aural: ASMR or white noise, 35dB
- Thermal: 18°C (cool for sleep)
- Scent: None (or optional chamomile)
- Duration: 30 minutes before bed
- Trigger: Evening routine, sunset, or manual selection

**Scene 5: "Energy Boost" (Afternoon slump, jet lag recovery)**

#### B. INTERACTION PATTERNS

**Design 3 core interactions**:

**Interaction 1: Scene Selection**
- User opens app → sees 6 scene thumbnails (visual + name + 2-line description)
- User taps scene → preview animation shows what will change
- User taps "Apply" → scene activates with 5-second transition
- User can customize individual parameters after scene applies
- Inspiration: Otto's preset approach

**Interaction 2: Transparency Feedback**
- System detects mood (via biometrics or user input)
- Shows notification: "I detected elevated stress (HRV: 65). Shall I activate Stress Recovery scene?"
- User can: Accept, Decline, or Customize before accepting
- After scene applies: "How's that working? Any adjustments?"
- Design principle: Users understand why system made recommendation

**Interaction 3: Emergency Override**
- Physical button or voice command: "Stop" or "Neutral"
- System instantly returns all parameters to neutral (neutral lighting, no sound, no scent)
- For privacy/safety: Always available, no questions asked
- Design principle: Users feel in control

## CRITICAL SUCCESS FACTORS

### 1. **Go Deep, Not Broad**
- Resist urge to "study everyone"
- Go 2 weeks with 8 consultants > 1 week with 20 random people
- Depth reveals patterns; breadth reveals noise

### 2. **Document Everything**
- Every observation, interview, metric
- Even seemingly small details matter
- Photos, videos, quotes, exact metrics
- Impossible to over-document

### 3. **Iterate on Insights**
- Don't wait until Phase 4 to synthesis
- Weekly: Review notes, update affinity map
- Let patterns emerge incrementally, not suddenly

### 4. **Embrace Constraints**
- Your constraints are your strengths
- "Olfactory is hard" = differentiation
- "Biometrics are imperfect" = design opportunity (transparency)
- "Privacy matters" = trust foundation

### 5. **Stay Human-Centered**
- Numbers matter (HRV, accuracy rates)
- But stories matter more (participant quotes, moments, emotions)
- Your case study will succeed on narrative + data, not data alone
