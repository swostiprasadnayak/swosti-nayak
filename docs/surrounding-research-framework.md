## 1. REFERENCE PROJECTS ANALYSIS

### PROJECT 1: Pharmacy Service Design - First Health
**Creators**: Sanika Yeola (Pune) & Sumit Bagal (Mumbai)  
**Duration**: 3 months (August-October)  
**Scope**: Comprehensive healthcare system redesign  
**Domain**: Service Design + UX Research + Information Architecture  

#### Research Phase Breakdown (August - 5 weeks)
```
WEEK 1:
- Desk Research (secondary research on healthcare systems)

WEEK 2-3:
- Current Scenario Analysis (how pharmacy operations work NOW)
- Market Study (landscape of existing pharmacy solutions)
- Contextual Enquiry - Pharmacy (field observations in pharmacies)

WEEK 3-4:
- Contextual Enquiry - Hospital (understanding doctor-side constraints)
- User Interviews with Patients & Customers (direct user feedback)

WEEK 4-5:
- Ethnography & Field Study (immersive observation of real behaviors)
- Competitive Analysis (landscape research)
```

#### Key Research Artifacts Created
1. **System Journey Loop** - Maps all touchpoints across: Patient → Doctor → Pharmacy → Payment → Medicine Delivery
   - Shows 3 parallel journeys: In-store, Via Mobile App, Via Telepharmacy
   - Identifies "Non-Available Medicine" as critical failure point
   - Maps all stakeholder interactions

2. **Purpose Mapping** - Stakeholder responsibility mapping showing:
   - Doctor (roles & responsibilities)
   - Pharmacist (detailed process flows)
   - Patient (touchpoints & needs)
   - All interconnected workflows

3. **User Personas** (3 main types):
   - **Patient**: Characteristics, pain points, expectations
   - **Pharmacist**: Daily challenges, needs, goals
   - **Doctor**: Integration requirements, workflow needs

4. **Service System Map** - Shows ecosystem relationships and data flows
5. **Journey Map** - Patient journey through entire healthcare experience
6. **Service Blueprint** - Detailed backend systems needed to support frontend experience
7. **Opportunity Mapping** - Problem → Solution space visualization
8. **"How Might We?" Framework** - Innovation prompts derived from research

#### Problems Identified
- **Economic Constraints**: Limited payment options, affordability concerns
- **Over-the-Counter (OTC) Issues**: Customers buying medicines without prescriptions
- **Availability**: Specific medicines not stocked; pharmacists refer to competitors
- **Limited Health Literacy**: Customers lack understanding of pharmacy advice
- **Transportation**: Rural areas have limited access; poor pharmacy accessibility
- **Casual Loop Analysis**: Showed how problems perpetuate (e.g., low trust → avoidance → worse health outcomes)

#### Design Solution Output
**First Health Prescription Software** - System connecting:
- Doctor portal (generate digital prescriptions)
- Pharmacy portal (receive, fulfill, counsel)
- Patient app (track prescriptions, health records, wellness products)
- Payment integration
- Delivery logistics

### PROJECT 2: Starbucks UX Research
**Creators**: Sumit Bagal (Mumbai) & Shresth Chauhan (New Delhi)  
**Scope**: Customer experience redesign for Starbucks stores  
**Domain**: UX Research + Interaction Design + Spatial Design  

#### Research Phase (Contextual Inquiry)
```
METHODOLOGY: Contextual Inquiry (Observational Research)

SAMPLE SIZE:
- 16 visits to Starbucks locations
- 6 days of observation across multiple visits
- 10 females, males observed
- 5 new customers, 11 regular customers
- Multiple locations across Bengaluru, Pune & Delhi

OBSERVATION APPROACH:
- Direct observation in natural context (not lab)
- Interviews conducted during/after observation
- Focus: Understanding user journey from entry → order → consumption → exit
- Noted: Time spent, decision points, friction areas, emotional states
```

#### Data Analysis Process
**Interpretation Phase**:
1. Transcribed all field notes and observations
2. Coded data by themes (emotions, behaviors, pain points, moments of delight)
3. Color-coded insights (red=problems, yellow=opportunities, green=moments of joy)
4. Cross-referenced observations with user statements

**Affinity Mapping**:
- Clustered observations into patterns
- Identified recurring themes across users
- Mapped frequency of issues vs. importance

#### Key Research Artifacts
1. **Personas** (3 types):
   - **Kalindi Khurana, 25 yrs**: "Goal-oriented" coffee drinker (efficiency-focused)
   - **Abhishek Sharma, 21 yrs**: "Experience seeker" (aesthetic, social)
   - **Prabir Singh, 28 yrs**: "Routine-driven" regular (consistency, comfort)

2. **Revised Design Brief**: 
   - Clear problem statement derived from research
   - Scope and goals for redesign

3. **User Needs Models**:
   - Functional needs (product, speed, convenience)
   - Emotional needs (belonging, discovery, comfort)
   - Social needs (interaction, status, community)

4. **Store Models & Spatial Analysis**:
   - 3D floor plan analysis
   - Customer flow patterns
   - Dwell time heatmaps
   - Decision point mapping

5. **Journey Maps**: 
   - Moment-by-moment experience breakdown
   - Pain points and opportunities flagged at each stage

6. **Physical Evidence Documentation**:
   - Store photography
   - Customer interaction documentation
   - Environmental observations

#### Key Insights Discovered
- **Ordering friction**: Customers unsure about menu customization options
- **Waiting anxiety**: Lack of queue visibility → anxiety about wait times
- **Seating ambiguity**: Unclear which tables are "available" vs. "reserved"
- **First-time user confusion**: New customers didn't know payment/ordering systems
- **Emotional attachment**: Regular customers had strong place-based memories

## 2. METHODOLOGY FRAMEWORK FOR "SURROUNDING" PROJECT

### Phase 1: CONTEXT & CONSTRAINT RESEARCH (Weeks 1-2)

**Goal**: Understand the ambient computing landscape, technical constraints, and user contexts

#### Research Activities
```
A. TECHNICAL FEASIBILITY RESEARCH
   ├─ Olfactory engineering deep-dive
   │  ├─ Interview hardware engineers working on scent delivery
   │  ├─ Analyze existing systems: Osmall, Scentronomy, DIY microfluidics
   │  ├─ Map latency, neutralization speed, maintenance burden
   │  └─ Document: Feasibility level, cost, scalability
   │
   ├─ Sensory synchronization research
   │  ├─ Review literature on sensory perception thresholds
   │  ├─ Test: Latency thresholds for desynchronization (motion sickness point)
   │  ├─ Map hardware latency by modality (visual, audio, thermal, scent, haptic)
   │  └─ Create "sensory sync budget" specifications
   │
   └─ Edge computing constraints
      ├─ Research on-device ML capabilities
      ├─ Benchmark: CPU/memory for biometric processing
      └─ Document: Privacy architecture options

B. EXISTING SMART HOME ECOSYSTEM RESEARCH
   ├─ Study existing ambient systems (Apple, Google, Philips Hue, Nest)
   ├─ Analyze: What works? What friction exists?
   ├─ Map: Current user expectations vs. reality
   └─ Identify: Untapped opportunities

C. MARKET & USER CONTEXT STUDY
   ├─ Survey: Who uses smart homes? Why? Pain points?
   ├─ Segments: Knowledge workers, high-stress professionals, wellness-focused users
   ├─ Context: Home, workspace, commute
   └─ Document: Addressable market size, willingness to pay
```

**Deliverables**:
- Technical feasibility scorecard
- Sensory sync budget spreadsheet
- Market opportunity brief
- Ecosystem landscape map

---

### Phase 2: USER RESEARCH & IMMERSION (Weeks 3-5)

**Goal**: Understand how people actually work, their stress patterns, cognitive load, and environmental preferences

#### Research Activities

```
A. CONTEXTUAL INQUIRY (Primary Research)
   
   TARGET USERS: Knowledge workers in high-cognitive-load jobs
   - Software engineers / Product managers
   - Writers / Designers
   - Surgeons / Medical professionals
   - Trading floor workers / Financial analysts
   
   SAMPLE SIZE: 12-15 participants across roles
   
   METHOD: In-situ observation + retrospective interviews
   
   ACTIVITIES:
   ├─ Observe actual work sessions (2-4 hours each)
   │  ├─ Workspace environment (lighting, temperature, noise)
   │  ├─ Breaks and rest patterns
   │  ├─ Mood/stress indicators (facial expression, posture, behavior)
   │  ├─ Task switching moments
   │  ├─ Cognitive load spikes
   │  └─ Environmental preferences expressed (opening windows, adjusting light, etc.)
   │
   ├─ Track biometric data (with consent)
   │  ├─ Heart rate variability (stress indicator)
   │  ├─ Time between breaks
   │  ├─ Fatigue signals
   │  └─ Productivity patterns
   │
   └─ Interview during/after sessions
      ├─ "What were you feeling during that task?"
      ├─ "How did the environment support/hinder your work?"
      ├─ "What would have made this easier?"
      └─ "When do you feel most creative/focused/drained?"

B. SENSORY PREFERENCE MAPPING
   
   ACTIVITIES:
   ├─ Implicit preference testing
   │  ├─ A/B test ambient conditions (lighting, temperature, sound)
   │  ├─ Measure: Task performance, self-reported comfort, stress levels
   │  └─ Track: Which users prefer which conditions
   │
   ├─ Interview about sensory associations
   │  ├─ "What smells/sounds/lighting remind you of focus?"
   │  ├─ "What environment helps you recover from stress?"
   │  ├─ "What's your ideal break environment?"
   │  └─ "Describe your most productive workspace ever"
   │
   └─ Retrospective mood-to-environment mapping
      └─ "When you felt X mood, what environment helped?"

C. FAILURE MODE RESEARCH
   
   ACTIVITIES:
   ├─ Scenario testing
   │  ├─ "What if the system misread your mood?"
   │  ├─ "What if scents mixed incorrectly?"
   │  ├─ "What if you got a priority alert during immersion?"
   │  ├─ "How would you override the system?"
   │  └─ "What's your privacy concern?"
   │
   └─ Diary study (1 week)
      ├─ Users log: Mood, environment, task type, productivity
      ├─ Track: False mood inferences they notice
      └─ Capture: Moments where system should have intervened

D. COMMUTE & CONTEXT HANDOFF RESEARCH
   
   ACTIVITIES:
   ├─ Map transit journey: Bedroom → Bathroom → Kitchen → Car → Office
   ├─ Document: Sensory shifts user experiences
   ├─ Identify: Transition friction points
   ├─ Test: Sensory "crossfade" preferences (how fast should environment shift?)
   └─ Interview: "How do you mentally transition between contexts?"
```

**Data Collection Tools**:
- Video/audio recordings (with consent)
- Biometric tracker data (heart rate, HRV)
- Mood/productivity logs (morning, midday, evening)
- Environmental preference surveys
- Photo documentation of current workspace solutions

**Deliverables**:
- 12-15 coded ethnographic case studies
- Sensory preference heatmaps
- Biometric → mood prediction model (preliminary)
- Failure scenario documentation
- Context transition analysis

### Phase 3: SYNTHESIS & PROBLEM FRAMING (Weeks 6-7)

**Goal**: Transform raw research into actionable insights and clearly articulated problems

#### Activities

```
A. AFFINITY MAPPING & PATTERN FINDING
   ├─ Code all observations (emotions, behaviors, pain points, opportunities)
   ├─ Cluster into themes:
   │  ├─ Stress recovery patterns
   │  ├─ Focus/flow triggers
   │  ├─ Environmental preferences by person & context
   │  ├─ Failure modes & safety concerns
   │  └─ Privacy & autonomy needs
   └─ Identify frequency: What patterns appear in most users?

B. USER PERSONAS (4-5 types)
   ├─ Persona 1: "The Burned-Out High-Performer"
   │  ├─ Goal: Recover without wasting time
   │  ├─ Pain: System guessing wrong mood
   │  ├─ Sensory preference: Calm, cool, minimal stimulation
   │  └─ Privacy concern: Tracking cognitive load feels invasive
   │
   ├─ Persona 2: "The Flow Seeker"
   │  ├─ Goal: Maximum focus for deep work
   │  ├─ Pain: Environmental distractions breaking concentration
   │  ├─ Sensory preference: Minimal surprises, predictable shifts
   │  └─ Privacy concern: Data about focus patterns = productivity surveillance
   │
   ├─ Persona 3: "The Wellness Optimizer"
   │  ├─ Goal: Optimize productivity & wellbeing through biohacking
   │  ├─ Pain: System being too conservative or too aggressive
   │  ├─ Sensory preference: Experimental, willing to try new conditions
   │  └─ Privacy concern: Health data security
   │
   ├─ Persona 4: "The Skeptic"
   │  ├─ Goal: Functionality without surveillance
   │  ├─ Pain: Invasive tracking, loss of agency
   │  ├─ Sensory preference: Manual control preferred, auto feels creepy
   │  └─ Privacy concern: Data being sold, profiling
   │
   └─ Persona 5: "The Commuter"
      ├─ Goal: Seamless experience across home/work/transit
      ├─ Pain: Jarring sensory transitions, lag between contexts
      ├─ Sensory preference: Predictable crossfades, familiar patterns
      └─ Privacy concern: Location tracking

C. SYSTEM JOURNEY LOOP (like Pharmacy project)
   
   Context: Home (Morning) → Workspace (Focus) → Commute → Home (Evening)
   
   For EACH context, map:
   ├─ Entry points (how does system know user entered?)
   ├─ Current mood/state detection (biometric + behavioral cues)
   ├─ Environmental adjustment sequence
   ├─ Sensory modalities engaged (visual, audio, thermal, scent, haptic)
   ├─ Duration of adjustment
   ├─ Touchpoints where user might override
   ├─ Exit/transition points
   └─ Failure scenarios at each stage

D. PROBLEM IDENTIFICATION
   
   Frame problems as:
   ├─ Users feel [emotion] when [situation] because [root cause]
   ├─ This matters because [business/user impact]
   │
   │ Example:
   │ "Users feel FRUSTRATED when trying to RECOVER from stress
   │ because the SYSTEM GUESSES WRONG about their emotional state,
   │ leading to inappropriate sensory interventions (e.g., energizing
   │ audio when they want calm). This matters because incorrect mood
   │ inference erodes trust and users disable the system."
   │
   └─ (5-7 key problems identified from research)

E. OPPORTUNITY MAPPING
   
   For each problem → generate "How Might We?" prompts
   ├─ HMW design a transparent mood inference system?
   ├─ HMW prevent sensory desynchronization?
   ├─ HMW give users agency over auto-ambient features?
   ├─ HMW detect emergencies and override immediately?
   └─ HMW ensure privacy while processing biometrics locally?
```

**Deliverables**:
- Affinity map (visual cluster of themes)
- 4-5 detailed user personas with research quotes
- System journey loops for each context (home, work, commute)
- Problem statement framework (5-7 key problems)
- Opportunity map with HMW questions
- Research insights brief (1-2 pages highlighting key findings)

### Phase 4: SOLUTION & SYSTEM DESIGN (Weeks 8-10)

**Goal**: Translate problems into system architecture, interaction models, and design specifications

#### Activities (Following Pharmacy Project Model)

```
A. SERVICE SYSTEM MAP
   ├─ Biometric sensors → What data collected?
   ├─ Edge ML processor → Mood/load prediction
   ├─ Orchestration engine → Priority hierarchies & override logic
   ├─ Hardware drivers → Lighting, audio, thermal, scent, haptic
   ├─ User interface → Override/explanation interface
   └─ Privacy layer → Local processing, user data control

B. ECOSYSTEM STAKEHOLDER MAPPING
   ├─ End users (knowledge workers)
   ├─ IoT device makers (Philips, Nanoleaf, etc.)
   ├─ Hardware manufacturers (scent devices, thermal units)
   ├─ ML researchers (biometric prediction accuracy)
   ├─ Privacy engineers (data security)
   └─ Regulatory bodies (health/privacy standards)

C. SERVICE BLUEPRINT
   ├─ Frontend: User-facing ambient experiences
   ├─ Middleware: Mood inference, transition logic
   ├─ Backend: Data processing, learning, preferences storage
   ├─ Support: Troubleshooting, override mechanisms
   └─ All failure scenarios mapped

D. INTERACTION DESIGN & WIREFRAMES
   
   Key screens/flows:
   ├─ Setup wizard (onboarding biometric tracking)
   ├─ Mood inference transparency (why did system make this adjustment?)
   ├─ Override interface (easy manual control)
   ├─ Context handoff screen (transition between spaces)
   ├─ Privacy settings (what data collected, where stored?)
   ├─ Emergency alert flow (override to neutral state)
   └─ Feedback loop (user teaches system their preferences)

E. PROTOTYPE & TESTING
   ├─ Build interactive prototype showing:
   │  ├─ Sensory transition sequences
   │  ├─ Mood inference explanations
   │  ├─ Override responsiveness
   │  └─ Cross-context handoffs
   │
   └─ Test with target users (5-7 sessions)
      ├─ "Does this feel invasive?"
      ├─ "Is the transition speed comfortable?"
      ├─ "Would you trust this system?"
      └─ "What would change your mind?"
```

**Deliverables**:
- Service system architecture diagram
- Ecosystem stakeholder map
- Service blueprint (complete with fail-safes)
- Interaction wireframes (key flows)
- Prototype (interactive demo of ambient shifts)
- Usability testing findings

## 3. CRITICAL SUCCESS FACTORS FOR "SURROUNDING"

Based on both reference projects, here are the non-negotiables:

### A. RESEARCH DEPTH
- **Pharmacy project**: 5 weeks of intensive research (desk, market, contextual, ethnographic)
- **Starbucks project**: 16 visits + deep observation + interviews
- **For Surrounding**: Minimum 4-6 weeks of actual user immersion
  - Can't fake this with surveys
  - Must observe real stress/recovery patterns
  - Must test sensory transitions with actual users

### B. ARTIFACTS THAT PROVE OWNERSHIP
Research alone isn't credible—you must CREATE:
- ✅ System Journey Loops (showing all touch points and parallel paths)
- ✅ User Personas derived FROM research (not generic)
- ✅ Service Blueprint (what happens behind the scenes?)
- ✅ "How Might We?" framework (problems → innovation)
- ✅ Failure scenario documentation (what breaks? how do you recover?)
- ✅ Privacy & ethics architecture (not an afterthought)

### C. CONSTRAINT DOCUMENTATION
Google loves seeing how you NAVIGATED constraints:
- ✅ Olfactory latency budget (why milliseconds matter)
- ✅ Biometric accuracy limitations (15% false positive rate → design implication)
- ✅ Hardware form factor tradeoffs (multi-chamber delivery = maintenance burden)
- ✅ Privacy-vs-personalization tension (addressed in system design)
- ✅ Business model constraints (cost of hardware, subscription model, etc.)

### D. NARRATIVE OF OWNERSHIP
In your portfolio, tell the story:
- "I identified that existing smart home systems don't address COGNITIVE LOAD recovery"
- "Through 16 immersive research sessions, I discovered that users DISTRUST mood inference"
- "This led me to design a TRANSPARENT system where the user always understands WHY an adjustment happened"
- "I had to push back on executives who wanted real-time cloud data—local processing won the privacy argument"
- "The system failed in 3 ways: [list them] → I designed fail-safes for each"
